# Changelog

## 1.2 — analysis with THD and spectrum, the DSP's true clock

Firmware 1.2, protocol 1 (new requests and events only), application 1.2, panel firmware 1.2.

- `diag heap` reports the ESP32's free heap, its largest free block and the lowest free since the start.
- A failed EEPROM read is tried again after the firmware frees the I2C bus (clocks out a byte a device was left sending, then a STOP); `diag boot` reports the reset reason, the bus lines at the start and the EEPROM reads that failed since.
- Docs: a front panel with its own supply half-powers the board's ESP32 through its cable while the board is unplugged, and the next power-up is not clean (the board silent until EN, or `eeprom-failed` in the SigmaStudio mode). Switch both together.
- Panel: the panel drives its TX line only while the board's TX line lives. It lets the line go (high-Z) within a tenth of a second of the board losing power, so it no longer feeds an unplugged board, and the board powers up cleanly with the panel on its own supply (5 of 5 replugs `power-on 0x01`). A board that loses power is noticed at once rather than after an unanswered request.
- **The DSP's clock is right now.** The ESP32 gave the ADAU1701 24.576 MHz instead of 12.288 MHz: on the ESP32 the MCLK pin carries the APLL itself, which the I2S driver runs at twice the port's MCLK. The DSP ran at twice its rating, and its converters made sporadic single-sample errors (about 4 % THD in a loopback, hidden from the Frequency Response by its band-pass). All three clock sources (the firmware's own, the generator's and synthesizer's, the radio's) now ask for 128 x fs, so 256 x fs reaches the DSP. The DSP samples at 48 kHz (at the stream's rate while the radio plays) and its core runs at 96 kHz, twice the sample rate, not four times; every DSP frequency is computed for that rate, also in the application's and the panel's equalizer curve. The loopback now measures 0.014 % THD, and the Frequency Response sweep is flat within 0.3 dB from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. A Frequency Response reference kept before this change was measured at the old rate: keep it again.
- **Response is now Analysis**, with three parts: **Frequency Response** (the sweep, as before), **THD** and **Spectrum**. The application, the panel, the docs and the SigmaStudio project carry the new name (`sigmastudio/Analysis`, `Analysis-Instructions.md`, `ADAU1701_Analysis.dspproj`, `DspImageAnalysis.h`); the protocol keeps the application's word `analyzer`, so scripts go on working. Its title in `apps` is "Analysis".
- **THD** in the analysis: `thd start` measures continuously what returns on the line input, about every 1.6 s: per channel the fundamental's level, THD (H2 to H10 below 20 kHz) and THD+N (20 Hz to 20 kHz), `harmonics` gives each harmonic, `spectrum points` a spectrum of up to 256 points; `! thd` reports each result. 8192 frames per FFT (esp-dsp), Blackman-Harris window, 4 captures averaged per channel, the tone on an FFT bin; about 82 KB of heap while it runs, in blocks small enough to fit after the radio has played (the radio leaves the heap at about 193 KB in smaller pieces). A K3→K2 loopback measures, at `level` -20 dB (-27 dBFS at the input), THD+N -66 dB (0.05 %) with the harmonics in the noise (THD below -81 dB); at 0 dB (-7 dBFS) THD+N -75 dB (0.017 %) and THD -76 dB, H2 -81 and H3 -78 dBc: the converters' own. New errors `busy` and `no-memory`.
- **Spectrum** in the analysis: `spectrum start` measures what comes on the line input without a tone of its own (the output and the tone stay as they are), with THD's FFT: per channel the RMS from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (`spectrum`, `! spectrum`), the five strongest peaks (`spectrum peaks`) and the spectrum (`spectrum points`). The board's open input: about -97.5 dBFS RMS, mains hum near 50 Hz around -105 dBFS. In the application **Measure: Spectrum** (RMS, the strongest peak per channel, the five peaks marked in the spectrum); on the panel a third view, **Spec**.
- How THD gets its samples: the analysis' DSP program also sends both line inputs, untouched, to the DSP's serial output (SerialOutL on DIG0, SerialOutR on DIG1: MP6, with the clocks on MP10 and MP11), which the ESP32 receives on its second I2S port (GPIO32, 13, 19). The firmware carries the new image; the band-pass cells and the readback settings moved and are taken over. `Analysis-Instructions.md` step 8 describes the serial output.
- The application's analysis page: **Measure: Frequency Response | THD**; THD shows the spectrum (the tone and its harmonics marked), THD and THD+N per channel in % and dB, and the harmonics. The panel's analysis page: a **Resp** / **THD** / **Spec** key in the plot's corner, Start/Stop, the spectrum below the keys; while THD or Spectrum measures, its values (THD, THD+N and level, or RMS and the strongest peak, per channel) take the tiles' place. A measurement started elsewhere shows itself. A sweep or THD started on one port now reports the output going on to the others.
- Docs brought up to date: the installation guide has a first measurement with the analysis (the board's own floor) and new troubleshooting rows; the manual has THD and Spectrum examples for the console and Python, the analysis' troubleshooting and a technical note on the line input's path to the ESP32; Building describes the DSP's clock and the measurement's memory. The overview drawing shows the front panel, on its own and as the PC's WiFi link (`tools/Make-Overview.py` draws it now), and the manual's front panel chapter carries the wiring and flashing too. The PDFs of the manual and the installation guide are built again.
- **Mechanical design**: the 3D-printed enclosure for the board and the front panel in `CAD` (STL: the box's base and lid, the panel's case and front plate), a manual chapter with photos of the built Audio-MiniLab (the box on its own, all the parts, the empty panel case with the connector between box and panel, the complete assembly) and pictures of the parts drawn from the STL files (`tools/Render-Cad.py`).
- Diagnostics for the serial output and the heap: `diag clocks` (the serial output's clocks and its data pin), `diag capture` (levels from the DC offset, frequency, DC), `diag record` (contiguous frames), `diag thd-test` (the THD analysis on a synthetic signal), `diag blocks` (the heap's free blocks). New error `no-clock`.

## 1.1 — front panel and WiFi control

Firmware 1.1, protocol 1 (new requests and events only), application 1.1, panel firmware 1.1.

- **A front panel** on an ESP32-2432S028R ("CYD") display board, on a three-wire cable to the board (docs/Panel.md): the home screen and a settings screen (pot control, power-up, brightness); each application's parameters as tiles with a value editor; the output switch; a knob strip in the knob colours whose knobs open the value their pot plays; the equalizer's curve; station keys with names and the stream title for the radio; Start/Stop, progress, a plot and a measurement cursor for the analyzer; section keys with the filter's and the envelopes' graphs for the synthesizer. It connects and reconnects by itself and follows changes made by the pots, the PC application and scripts live. `Flash-Panel.cmd` flashes it; the release carries its image.
- **WiFi control through the panel:** the panel joins the board's WiFi networks and takes the PC application on TCP port 7000 (`audio-dsp-lab.local`). The application's port list offers "Network: <address>", set up in the WiFi dialog's new Front panel section.
- **The board's panel port:** a second protocol port on UART2 (GPIO37 RX, GPIO4 TX, 115200 baud); a value one port sets reaches the other as an event. New: `wifi secrets` (panel port only), the `panel` request, and the `! wifi` and `! panel` events. The synthesizer's MIDI port no longer claims GPIO4.
- **The manual** has a chapter on the front panel, with screenshots taken from the panel itself (`tools/Capture-PanelScreen.ps1`).

## 1.0 — first public release

Firmware 1.0, protocol 1, application 1.0.

- Six applications for the Elektor Audio-DSP-FX board: generator, equalizer, internet radio, response analyzer, SigmaStudio mode, synthesizer.
- The generator makes its signal on the ESP32: sine, rectangle, triangle, saw, inverse saw and noise, band-limited, with a width for the rectangle and the triangle; its pots follow the other applications (P1 waveform, P2 frequency, P3 width, P4 amplitude).
- A line-based text protocol on the USB serial port (921600 baud) with self-describing parameters and events instead of polling.
- WiFi networks for the radio are entered from the PC and kept on the board; no credentials are compiled into the firmware.
- Windows application with one page per application, the board's pots shown live, stored settings, a protocol console and a built-in board simulator.
- The synthesizer page draws the filter's response and the two envelopes beside their dials, like the equalizer's curve.
- Three themes for the window: Classic, Studio dark and Studio light; the pot knobs in the knob-cap colours P1 blue, P2 green, P3 yellow, P4 red.
- Python client `tools/adl.py`; flash tool and release builder.

Audio-DSP-Lab grew out of the author's Audio-MiniLab, an instrument of a private lab automation system; this release is the standalone version, with its own protocol and PC application.
